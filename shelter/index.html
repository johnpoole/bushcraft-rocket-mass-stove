One-person shelter between three trees

A lean-to of poles, wattle and daub and a turf roof, framed on three living trees and one post, with the rocket mass stove inside. The stove's bench is the bed.

Site

  • Three healthy trees standing roughly in a right angle, about 3.2 m and 2.3 m apart, centre to centre. The front of the shelter faces south between two of them.
  • Trees with high crowns and no dead limbs over the shelter. Branches must be at least 2 m above the chimney top.
  • Level ground at least 25 m from the stream and well above its high-water mark. Cold air and fog settle along the stream at night.
  • The stream gives clay from its banks, sand from its bed and water for mixing. Keep stones from the stream away from the fire. Use them for the drain and the path.

The stove in this shelter

The stove stands by the post, in the corner farthest from the trees. The bench runs along the back wall and turns along the west wall. The flue leaves through the west wall to a chimney between the two west trees, clear of the roof. Open the stove simulator

Cooking and water

  • The cooktop is the flat stone on top of the dome. Flame from the riser strikes its underside, so it is hottest over the riser and cooler toward the edges. Boil or fry in the middle and slide a pot to the edge to simmer.
  • The counter is a split log laid flat side up on stakes driven into the floor, 80 cm high. One leg runs along the east wall for food prep. The other runs along the front wall and holds the water basin.
  • The basin is a length of log hollowed by laying hot coals on it and scraping out the char, over and over. Fill it from the stream and keep a pot of water at the edge of the cooktop for hot water.
  • Throw used water out well away from the stream, not into the drain.

Build order

  1. Clear to mineral soil. Cut a shallow ditch along the uphill side, behind the back wall, to turn rainwater around the shelter.
  2. Set the post at the fourth corner, one more halfway along the back, and one each side of the door. Lash a beam from the corner post to the north-west tree at 2.0 m, and a plate between the two south trees at 1.2 m. Pad the bark under each lashing with moss or bark and wrap it loosely, so the tree can sway and grow. Loosen the lashings every year.
  3. Lay rafters every 40 cm from beam to plate. Cover them with bark sheets lapped like shingles, then 10–15 cm of turf, grass side up. Where the roof passes a trunk, leave a hand's width and close it with a loose bark collar.
  4. Under the roof, dig a pit 30 cm deep for the stove core in the post corner and line it with stone. Dig a gravel-filled drain 50 cm deep from the bottom of the pit, out under the east wall and downhill. Build the stove in the pit, then the bench, as in the build guide. Build the chimney in stone and cob to 1 m, then stand a hollow log lined thick with clay on top, up to 2.35 m, and lash it to a post driven in beside it. The channel climbs out of the pit into the bench. Leave 10 cm of light clay between the back wall and the stove and bench.
  5. Lay a dry stone plinth 30 cm high under the walls. Drive stakes into the ground and weave hazel or willow rods between them. Stop the weave 5 cm short of each trunk.
  6. Daub both sides of the wattle with cob. Daub it thick where it faces the stove. Fill the gap at each trunk with clay mixed with moss, which gives as the tree moves.
  7. Leave an air inlet low in the front wall and a vent high in the back wall. Hang a hide or blanket in the door.
  8. Drive four stakes for the counter and lay split log halves across them. Burn and scrape a log for the basin.
  9. Dry everything, then fire the stove small at first as the guide says.

Safety

  • Keep the low air inlet and the high vent open whenever the stove burns. If you get a headache, dizziness or nausea, get outside.
  • Let the fire burn out before you sleep. The bench holds the heat through the night.
  • Keep bedding, clothes and the woodpile off the cooktop and away from the feed tube.
  • The feed tube opens just above the floor. Lay a flat hearth stone in front of it to catch sparks and ash, and keep the floor around it clear.
  • Look up before you build and after every storm. Pull down any dead branch over the shelter.
  • Trees sway in the wind. After a storm, check the lashings and the clay seals where the walls meet the trunks.
  • Keep branches cut back at least 2 m above the chimney top. Sparks land in them otherwise.
  • Check the clay lining inside the chimney log each season. If it has cracked or the wood has charred, reline it.
