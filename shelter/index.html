One-person shelter against a rock face

A lean-to of poles, wattle and daub and a turf roof, built against a south-facing rock face with the rocket mass stove inside. The stove's bench is the bed.

Site

  • A rock face that faces south, so the sun warms it and the shelter opens to the light.
  • Solid rock with no loose slabs or cracks overhead, no dead trees above, and a face that stays dry. Water running down the face will run into the shelter.
  • Level ground at least 25 m from the stream and well above its high-water mark. Cold air and fog settle along the stream at night.
  • The stream gives clay from its banks, sand from its bed and water for mixing. Keep stones from the stream away from the fire. Use them for the drain and the path.

The stove in this shelter

The bench runs along the rock and turns along the west wall. The flue leaves through the west wall to a chimney that stands clear of the roof. Open the stove simulator

Cooking and water

  • The cooktop is the flat stone on top of the dome. Flame from the riser strikes its underside, so it is hottest over the riser and cooler toward the edges. Boil or fry in the middle and slide a pot to the edge to simmer.
  • The counter is a split log laid flat side up on stakes driven into the floor, 80 cm high. One leg runs along the east wall for food prep. The other runs along the front wall and holds the water basin.
  • The basin is a length of log hollowed by laying hot coals on it and scraping out the char, over and over. Fill it from the stream and keep a pot of water at the edge of the cooktop for hot water.
  • Throw used water out well away from the stream, not into the drain.

Build order

  1. Clear to mineral soil. Dig a gravel-filled drain 50 cm deep along the foot of the rock face, so it runs below the stove pit, then out under the west wall and downhill.
  2. Set the posts: three against the rock, four along the front. Lash a beam across the back posts at 2.0 m and a plate across the front posts at 1.2 m.
  3. Lay rafters every 40 cm from beam to plate. Cover them with bark sheets lapped like shingles, then 10–15 cm of turf, grass side up.
  4. Where the roof meets the rock, tuck bark into the rock and seal the joint with a thick bead of clay.
  5. Under the roof, dig a pit 30 cm deep for the stove core in the corner by the rock and line it with stone. Build the stove in the pit, then the bench and the chimney, as in the build guide. The channel climbs out of the pit into the bench. Leave 10 cm of light clay between the rock and the stove and bench.
  6. Lay a dry stone plinth 30 cm high under the walls. Weave hazel or willow rods between the posts above it.
  7. Daub both sides of the wattle with cob. Daub it thick where it faces the stove.
  8. Leave an air inlet low in the front wall and a vent high at the back under the roof. Hang a hide or blanket in the door.
  9. Drive four stakes for the counter and lay split log halves across them. Burn and scrape a log for the basin.
  10. Dry everything, then fire the stove small at first as the guide says.

Safety

  • Keep the low air inlet and the high vent open whenever the stove burns. If you get a headache, dizziness or nausea, get outside.
  • Let the fire burn out before you sleep. The bench holds the heat through the night.
  • Keep bedding, clothes and the woodpile off the cooktop and away from the feed tube.
  • The feed tube opens just above the floor. Lay a flat hearth stone in front of it to catch sparks and ash, and keep the floor around it clear.
  • Wind off the rock face can push smoke back down the chimney. If it does, make the chimney taller.
