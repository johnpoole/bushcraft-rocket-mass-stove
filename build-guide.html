Rocket mass stove from bushcraft materials

This is a rocket stove joined to a mass of earth, clay and stone that soaks up the heat. It burns small sticks hot and clean. The hot exhaust runs through a thick clay-and-stone bench before it leaves, and the bench gives off the heat for hours. Everything below can be gathered or made on site; no metal is needed.

This page is the design reference for the stove in the one-person shelter between three trees: how it works and every size. The step-by-step instructions are in Instructions, where each job lists the tools, materials and skills it needs and links to the jobs it relies on. The sizes there are filled in from the same design, so the two cannot disagree.

Section drawing

How it works

 feed tube ──► burn tunnel ──► heat riser (insulated chimney) ──► dome (hot exhaust drops)
                                                                       │
             chimney ◄── channel through the bench (heat goes into the mass) ◄──┘

The riser is insulated, so it gets very hot and pulls hard, like a turbo. That strong pull drives the exhaust through the long bench channel.

Sizing

The feed tube, burn tunnel, riser and all the channels have the same cross-section, 11 × 11 cm, all the way through. The other sizes below were chosen within the allowed ranges so the stove keeps the most heat and still starts well from cold and with damp wood.

PartSizeAllowed range
Feed tube (vertical, sticks drop in)11 × 11 cm, 30 cm tallabout 30 cm tall
Burn tunnel (horizontal)11 × 11 cm, 40 cm long30–40 cm long
Heat riser11 × 11 cm inside, 110 cm tall80–120 cm, and at least twice the tunnel length
Riser insulation8 cm of light clay around a 3 cm refractory wall8–10 cm
Gap from riser top to cooktop6 cm5–8 cm
Gap from riser to dome wall5 cmabout 5 cm
Bench channel11 × 11 cm, about 3.9 m, 2 bendsup to 3–4 m, as few bends as you can
Mass over the channel20 cm15–25 cm
Chimney11 × 11 cm, top 2.35 m above the floor, stone and cob all the way uptop at least 60 cm above the riser top and 60 cm above the roof beside it

If you undersize any section, it will choke the whole system and smoke will come out of the feed tube.

How the dimensions are measured

They are all inside measurements: the open space the fire and gas move through. Wall thickness goes on the outside of these numbers, so the formers you pack clay around should be these sizes.

DimensionMeasured
11 × 11 cm cross-sections (feed tube, burn tunnel, riser, channel, chimney)Inside wall to inside wall, the clear opening
Feed tube height (30 cm)From the burn tunnel floor up to the top of the feed opening
Burn tunnel length (40 cm)Along the inside floor, from the inside of the feed tube's back wall to the inside of the riser's far wall
Riser height (110 cm)From the burn tunnel floor to the top lip of the riser
Riser-top-to-cooktop gap (6 cm)Clear air gap from the riser's top lip to the underside of the cooktop stone
Riser-to-dome-wall gap (5 cm)Clear air gap from the outside of the riser to the inside of the dome wall
Bench mass over channel (20 cm)From the top of the channel's inside to the bench surface
Chimney top (2.35 m)From the shelter floor, which is level with the burn tunnel floor

Measuring the riser from the tunnel floor and the tunnel along its inside floor keeps the riser-to-tunnel ratio honest.

If a wall ends up bumpy, make sure the narrowest point is still at least 11 × 11 cm. One pinch point chokes the draft as badly as undersizing the whole run. Cob also shrinks a little as it dries, so it's fine to make the formers about 1 cm oversize.

Build it

Each link opens the instruction, with what it needs and the jobs it calls. They run in this order within the season plan; the season schedule shows when each one falls.

  1. Build a test burner first, outside and away from the shelter, to prove your clay and proportions draw.
  2. Stage one: a working stove — base, burn tunnel and feed tube, riser, dome and cooktop, duct, chimney, seal.
  3. Dry it and fire it for the first time.
  4. Prepare for the blocks and make clay blocks in batches from the first days.
  5. Stage two: the bench — thin lifts of stone in mortar over the duct, a block face and top, then drying.
  6. Until the bench is done, sleep warm on hot stones.

Using it

  • Fire it in the evening, every day.
  • Clean it out.
  • Stove and carbon monoxide safety.
