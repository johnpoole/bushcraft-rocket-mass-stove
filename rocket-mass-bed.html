Alone-style build · no metal · axe, saw, knife, pot

Rocket mass bed, cut through the middle

A 15 cm rocket stove whose exhaust runs under a single bed of clay and stone. All dimensions are in centimetres and measure the inside clear opening; wall thickness goes outside them. Heights are from the burn tunnel floor. This is a concept drawing that hasn't been test-built, so build and fire the core first.

  • Fired clay: 1 clay to 1–2 sand, no fibre, hardened by the first fires
  • Fired clay bricks, made first in a pit fire
  • Light clay insulation: clay slip + sawdust, dry grass or ash
  • Cob: 1 clay to 2–3 sand, plus fibre; pack dense stone into the bed
  • Flat stone slab (dry-land stone only)
  • Dry stone foundation
  • Woven withy former, burns out
  • Fire and exhaust path

Build order

  1. Build in autumn, before the ground freezes, while you still have reserves to spend.
  2. Dig clay, test a few patties in the fire, and pit-fire a dozen bricks for the tunnel floor.
  3. Lay the dry stone foundation across the whole footprint.
  4. Build the feed tube, burn tunnel and riser. Weave the riser former from dry withies and plaster it inside and out.
  5. Test it as a cooking stove. Only carry on once it draws cleanly with no smoke back up the feed tube.
  6. Build the dome and cap, then the stone channel, the bed mass, and the chimney through the roof.
  7. Dry with small fires for several days, raising the heat slowly. Seal every crack with slip as it appears.

Before you sleep beside it

  • You'll have no CO alarm. Keep a vent in the shelter, seal cracks the day they appear, and never close the feed tube while it burns.
  • Fire it hard for 1–2 hours in the evening, then let it go out before sleeping. The bed holds the heat.
  • If you wake with a headache, dizziness or nausea, get outside at once.
  • Rake ash from the tunnel and the cleanout plug every few days.
